## Rules stated
- Modern gaming videos are storytelling and challenges, not "best moments and fails"; viewers need a clear narrative to follow [said 00:00, 01:03].
- Plan before you record: what happens, why it happens (the stakes), and how it will unfold [said 01:43].
- Record with OBS using multiple audio tracks so your voice is not mixed into the game audio [said 02:25].
- Voice-over: state the interesting or relevant thing; a voice-over does not rescue boring footage, so cherry-pick only the important moments and throw away a lot of gameplay [said 03:27-03:48].
- The intro must tell the what, why and how; use effects, animations, slides and motion graphics; "smoothness is the key", meaning good visual flow and sliding keyframes [said 04:09-04:50]. Aim: the viewer thinks "if this is the quality I get now, I'll stay for the whole video" [said 05:10]. An intro that leaves the audience unclear what game, what challenge or why it matters loses them [said 05:10].
- Zooms: use them to move from point A to point B and to focus on important parts; "the smoother the better", done with keyframes or a preset [said 05:30-05:50].
- Subtitles: use them to highlight a humorous moment or a hard-to-hear line; "get creative" with the text [said 05:50-06:10].
- Memes: "don't overdo" them; compared to sending a friend a million reels, send one amazing one now and again [said 06:10-06:30].
- Transitions help smoothness and keep momentum between cuts, but too many get jarring; Premiere's default transitions look "okay" [said 06:30-06:50].
- Music: "probably the most important"; it sets the vibe and the pace. Use similar music across one video or stick to one game's soundtrack; avoid anything that gets the video demonetised [said 06:50-07:12].
- Sound effects: "arguably just as important as music"; add one to every major thing that happens: swishes on transitions, pops when text or graphics animate in [said 07:12-07:32]. Open the gain control (G in Premiere) and set it every time; do not let sound effects be too loud [said 07:32].
- On "retention editing": it has picked up a stigma; people no longer like the relentless cut-rate style (the auto-caption reads "one cup per minute", so the exact unit is unclear), and there is a preference for clear, smooth videos. Leave space to slow down and talk; do not cut out every dead space; let personality show [said 07:52-08:12].
- Review: after the render, take a day off, then watch as the audience, asking whether the intro informs, whether you are on board with the story, whether it entertains, whether the people are interesting, and whether it holds attention for the whole duration [said 08:32-08:53].
- Presets he sells (zoom pack, animation presets) are promoted three times [said 04:50, 05:50, 09:34]; sponsored/self-promotional, not craft.
