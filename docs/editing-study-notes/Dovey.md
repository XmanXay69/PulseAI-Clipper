## Rules stated
- Tell more stories: practise by putting everyday events (a hike) into story form, with context, a goal and a result, instead of just stating facts [said 01:01-04:03].
- Five structural beats: (1) history/setup, (2) the challenge (goal + what you're up against), (3) complications (things that push you away from or closer to the goal), (4) payoff (did you achieve it, not, or in between), (5) change (how it changed you) [said 04:23-08:08].
- Setup = put the viewer in the scene with just enough context; it can be a quick line or longer if they need history [said 04:23-05:03].
- Challenge: time pressure is an easy, strong challenge; he cites 24-hour and 48-hour challenges and his own "three days" Pony Express overland trip, which became the through line, with each roadblock setting him back [said 05:03-06:26]. His hike example: about 1,800 ft of climbing over a couple of miles with only about an hour of daylight [said 02:42-03:03].
- Complications are the roadblocks; plan some when outlining, but they mostly happen naturally while filming, so spend time filming them so they become story elements [said 06:26-07:28].
- Payoff must resolve what was set up at the start [said 07:28-07:48].
- Change: in vlogs it will rarely be dramatic, but look for how the journey made you feel, because the audience goes through it with you [said 07:48-08:29].
- On YouTube the beats have to come much faster than in film: a MrBeast video gives the setup, the context and the challenge "within the first 30 seconds" [said 09:11-09:31].
- Consume more stories, fewer tutorials; then write the five beats down for stories you watch (he offers a fill-in PDF) and notice when a video lacks structure [said 10:54-12:59].
- Be a visual storyteller: in vlogs we often talk to camera, but it is often better to show what is happening than tell it; use wide shots, close-ups and mediums; a quick story with setup, journey and payoff can be told in a few visuals (his example: daughter on a swing) [said 13:19-14:42].
- For a vlog of your day: set a goal, think about what will stand in the way, and note complications, instead of just turning the camera on and shooting whatever happens [said 15:02-15:42].
- Attention is divided, so there is not much time to grab and hold the viewer [said 00:00-00:20].
- Numbers he gives: "five ways", five beats, 86% of viewers not subscribed (plug), 30 seconds, three days, 24/48-hour challenges, 1,800 ft [said as timestamped above].
