## Rules stated
- Challenge of the video: hold attention with 15 techniques "without using a single clip of b-roll" [said 00:00-00:20]. (He lists about ten tips in the 4 minutes that follow.)
- Slow keyframed zoom-in on a talking head: the same line shown once flat and once with a slow zoom, and the zoomed one feels about half as long; he says it works because the shot's movement engages; he uses it "everywhere" in YouTube edits; the example is "boring for about 5 seconds" [said 01:03-01:45]. This is his perception claim, not measured.
- Quick keyframed zoom-in as a hook at the start of an idea, or to emphasise a crucial point [said 01:45-02:06].
- Punch-in: switch from a wide shot to a close-up to highlight a reaction or a key detail [said 02:06].
- Cutting between camera angles: make sure there is a motion to cut to (his example is a head turn) and cut on that motion [said 02:06-02:26].
- Premiere's basic 3D effect for virtual camera tilts ("a screen within a screen"): use only on occasion [said 02:26].
- Cut to the beat of a fast-paced song; use either cuts or punch-ins; each visual cut is "legitimised" by an audio cue on the beat [said 02:26-03:07].
- Music: you should hear a lot of it; he normally changes the background music at every new topic to reset attention; in this very dense video he would not change it that often [said 03:07-03:48].
- Every visual cue should have an audio cue: a whoosh on a zoom-in, a muffled beep on text popping in [said 03:48-04:09].
- Black-and-white filter for flashbacks or dramatic segments [said 04:09].
- Generative fill (Photoshop) to swap a static shot's background so it stays visually interesting without b-roll [said 04:29].
- Caveat from the creator: none of it matters if the A-roll was not cut correctly first; he points to another video of his on the correct way to cut talking-head footage [said 04:29-04:49].
