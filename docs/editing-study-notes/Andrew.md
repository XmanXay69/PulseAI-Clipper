## Rules stated
- The "shot sequence": gather several clips of one action, cut every clip to the same length, and stitch them so the motion flows from one into the next. It moves the story forward fast and doubles as a hook [said 00:22-00:43].
- Visuals are only half the picture; sound design completes it. He lists the sound effects he uses by name instead of selling a pack [said 01:04-01:27]. Names on screen: "flute riser", "designed snow step", "city ambience", "air passing" [seen 01:20-01:26].
- Music is the "backbone": it sets rhythm, pace and emotion [said 01:27].
- Avoid lo-fi (stale, overused); prefer jazz, house or something cinematic that tells the story under the visuals [said 01:27-01:48].
- Choose the music before filming so you know the rhythm you are shooting for [said 01:48].
- When choosing a track, check beats per minute (how fast it will feel) and look for dips in the track; he cuts scenes at the dips so the scene change feels natural and the music does not have to be chopped [said 02:09-02:30]. No BPM value is given.
- Colour grading supplies the "flavour" and is "stopping people in their scroll" [said 02:30]. Light first: more light means more information; add small practical lights in the background; put coloured objects in frame; then apply a one-click grade [said 02:50-03:10].
- "Less is more": the whole process is that simple; creativity does not need to be unusual [said 03:31].
- Closing challenge: story in three chapters; per chapter a visual sequence that pairs with two to three songs; aesthetic framing so viewers feel it is beautiful [said 04:34].
- Claim: only about 20% of people watch a video like this to the end [said 04:55]. Not evidence of editing craft.
