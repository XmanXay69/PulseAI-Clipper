## Rules stated
- Footage cut-down: play and pause with space, press C (razor) and click to cut, delete unwanted clips, ripple-delete the gap so no blanks remain; L speeds up playback; audio peaks help find where interesting things happen [said 02:01-02:41].
- Do subtitles first (his personal habit): type text with Ctrl+T, centre-align it, Ctrl-drag it to the bottom of the screen [said 02:41-03:23].
- Caption look: a bold, easy-to-read font (American Captain, as named), a black stroke with size 10, plus a black shadow, so the text stands out and is easy to read [said 03:23].
- Music matters because it gives the clips a rhythm, underlines the story and shapes the emotion; he demonstrates the same cat clip made innocent or sinister just by swapping the music [said 03:23-04:05].
- Music licensing is pitched as the way to use mainstream songs without a claim and still monetise; over one million tracks, sound effects included, free trial and a discount on the first track (this is the sponsor segment) [said 04:05-05:08].
- Levels: set music and sound effects to a sensible level either with the clip's level in Effect Controls or with Audio Gain (G key); keep the audio in the green range of the meter [said 05:08-05:29].
- Save "like every five seconds" [said 05:29].
- Zooms are great especially in gaming videos because they highlight the important part of a clip [said 05:50].
- Zoom recipe: stopwatch on Scale and Position, move forward five frames, raise Scale and shift X and Y so the target sits in the middle, then add a whoosh sound [said 06:11].
- Transitions: drag in Premiere's built-in ones and try them, but do not overdo it, a plain cut can be all you need [said 06:52].
- Audio transitions: a crossfade when one song leads straight into another; fade a song or sound effect in by setting its level to the lowest, skipping forward "a couple frames" and setting 0 dB; he uses this on almost all of his sound effects and music for smoothness [said 06:52-07:33].
- Export: H.264, full HD 1920x1080 at 60 frames per second recommended (he shows 4K for this video); set the in/out range with the mini timeline, "scale to fit" [said 07:33-08:16].
- The more time spent editing, the faster and more confident you get [said 08:16].
- No numbers or rules on hook length, re-hooks, cut rate or zoom count (the "30 times already" line at 05:50 is a joke, see below).

## Caveats
- A beginner tutorial for Premiere Pro, not a pro gaming edit; there is no timeline of a finished gaming video, only demos.
- Roughly 17% of the runtime is promotion, and the music-licensing claims are sponsor copy; the editing rules are not obviously affected.
- Zoom numbers (135 to 300) come from a few small frames read at 4x; the 5-frame figure is spoken, but the sequence frame rate is unknown.
- Hard-cut rates here are inflated by animated captions, mascot sprites and overlay transitions; the 0.35 and 0.15 thresholds both pick those up.
- Audio loudness and the sound-effect rate were not measured; my pop-up and mascot counts are by eye from 50 scene-biased frames, not a uniform sample.
