## Rules stated
- Keep raw gameplay and voice recordings in one dedicated folder and never touch them while editing [said 00:24].
- Sync gameplay and voice, then set the sequence start by scrubbing to the start of the recording and pressing I [said 00:44].
- Skim the whole 2-3 hours of gameplay and pull the best bits; "look for the spikes in audio", because "loud equals funny" [said 01:04-01:24]. Be generous when selecting at first and filter harder on a second pass [said 01:24].
- Two pacing styles: longer "authentic" stretches with room to breathe between words, or "a more reasonably paced video which will engage the audience faster". He prefers the faster one because audiences today "need it fast" [said 01:44-02:05].
- Cut a scream short: cut on the scream or just after its first start, so the viewer knows it is coming and the cut itself is the joke [said 02:05-02:25].
- Strip anything unneeded from voice clips: background noise and stray laughter that ruins the gameplay; adding the voice afterwards is allowed [said 02:46].
- Position and scale (punch-ins) emphasise comedy and point at what the audience should see; he compares it to close-ups in films [said 03:27-03:47].
- But keep wide and establishing shots, so the audience knows where they are in the game space; "don't go too crazy" with zooms [said 03:47].
- Watch the cut back "about 10 to 15 times" [said 03:47].
- Memes: build a library from YouTube meme playlists (videos, sound effects and songs); find "unmemed" moments and drop a meme there to see how it works [said 04:07-04:28].
- Sound is "huge" in comedy; including the right sounds is essential [said 04:48]. He keeps a standard pack of his most-used effects [said 05:08].
- Background music is a time filler to rescue boring gameplay and, in some cases, to enhance the mood [said 05:08]. He claims copyright can be ignored until monetising; that is his stated attitude, not a rule PULSE should copy [said 05:28].
- Subtitles last, optional, and tracked on to moving heads ("tracked and looking smooth") [said 06:13-06:34].
- Outro is the last thing seen: brand style, channel name, and space for the YouTube end cards [said 06:55].
- Save often (Ctrl/Cmd-S) [said 05:53].

## Against today's playbook
- retention.cutsPerMinute 12 / averageShotLength 5: this video measures 22-33 cuts/min and 1.8-2.6 s shots; his own tutorials run at about twice to three times the playbook rate. [measured 20-420 s]
- retention.popupsPerMinute 1.0: he recommends adding a meme to every "unmemed" moment [said 04:28] and the video shows a change about every 1.2 s [estimated]. The playbook's 1 per minute looks very low for his comedy style. Meme density, not text pop-ups, drives this.
- retention principle "Cut the breath, keep the reaction": he cuts the scream early so the viewer anticipates it [said 02:05]. This is a refinement for screams and jump-scares: trim the end of the scream, not its start. Worth a rule.
- retention.maxZoomsPerMinute 8: he warns against too many zooms and wants wides kept [said 03:47] but gives no number; consistent with a cap, no number support.
- retention.maxCaptionWords 4 and positionY 0.82: pop-up text of 2-4 words matches; labels sit about 0.85 down; the subtitle shadow values (135 degrees, 2.3, 12.4, 19) are mild. [seen]

- Fps is never stated, so frame values and clip widths are approximate. Auto-captions garble some words. The "no need to worry about copyright" remark is his attitude; PULSE should not adopt it.
- I did not listen to the audio: "heard" tags mean the auto-captions of his speech; music and sound-effect presence comes only from caption [Music] markers and from what he says, and levels were not measured.
