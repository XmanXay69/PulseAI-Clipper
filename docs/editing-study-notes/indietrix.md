## Rules stated
- Start with the voiceover on a fresh timeline: keep only the takes you want, then play it back; stuck together it "sounds terrible", so space the clips out by ear [said 01:03-01:24].
- There is no fixed rule for the gap between voice clips; listen to it. A longer pause after an important point, short pauses for a quick list; it depends on context [said 01:24-01:48].
- Lay the film footage on top: set in and out points on the source and drag only that portion, then fine-trim on the timeline [said 01:48-02:11].
- Expect to shuffle the voiceover around once the clips go in (scenes are longer, shorter or in another order than remembered); do not treat the script order as fixed [said 02:33].
- Mute the film's own audio channel while laying in clips [said 02:33].
- Fill blank space on the timeline by asking "what is the most direct, clear way to explain this point with visuals?"; it is a video essay, not an essay, so explain visually where possible [said 03:17].
- Let film clips play as they are; avoid pausing, rewinding and drawing red circles on framing [said 03:38-03:59].
- Titles, graphics and archive photos or posters are used "sparingly", so each one lands harder; use them when a clip cannot make the point (production history) [said 00:42, 04:23].
- Music: think about how it changes the tone (jazzy music under a grief essay sends the wrong message); not necessarily the whole way through; prefer licensed music with a credit at the end for copyright reasons [said 05:06-05:55].
- Film sound: always keep the sound of an actual scene shown; additionally use film sound effects where no dialogue is playing, so the voiceover stays clear; pull selected audio from the muted film channel down to a new track [said 06:17-06:42].
- Final checks: watch again on speakers and on headphones; can you hear everything, do the cuts flow, are the clips right, are titles spelled correctly; then export and watch the export again [said 07:02-07:27].
- No numbers (seconds, dB, percentages) are given for pause length, music level, title length or clip length.
