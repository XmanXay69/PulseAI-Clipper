## Rules stated
- Cut on the loud high point of the audio: chopping in the middle of a laugh or a scream is "usually" funny [said 02:06].
- Be ruthless on the rough cut: watch it again and again and delete bits that only you find funny, because the viewer must be "in on the joke" [said 02:28]. A friend's rule he repeats: an inside joke for six people loses the rest of the world, so generalise it [said 02:49].
- Order of work: rough cut ("super cut") first, then "layers and layers of polish"; the first polish step is always position and scale [said 03:12].
- Every crop (zoom/reframe) must be purposeful, a way to direct the viewer's attention [said 04:35].
- A slight constant crop/drift is also fine just to add visual movement, as discussion channels and vloggers do [said 04:55].
- Keyframe a zoom by setting a keyframe, moving "a couple of frames" forward and changing position/scale again [said 03:54]. Turn motion blur on for zooms because it "adds to the smoothness" [said 04:14].
- Subtitles: build one text, then Alt-drag (or Ctrl-drag / copy-paste) to duplicate it rather than rebuild each one [said 05:37, 07:19]. In HitFilm he keyframed the text so it pops [said 06:38].
- Transitions "can make or break the pacing"; pick one theme (his example: camera-glitch/VHS effect on a horror video) [said 07:19].
- The whip pan is the most common all-purpose transition. Premiere recipe: adjustment layer dragged to "about 12 frames" long, Directional Blur on it, keyframed at beginning, middle and end, plus a Push transition between the two clips [said 07:40-08:26]. DaVinci: same idea, push transition plus an adjustment clip with OpenFX Directional Blur, border type Replicate, blur strength highest in the middle and lowest at both ends [said 08:46-09:07].
- Where a program has no easy whip pan (Vegas), use a fast push and "rely on the sound effects to carry it" [said 08:26].
- Rule of thumb: keep transitions punchy; if the viewer does not notice them you did well; get a friend to watch and ask if anything stood out [said 09:27].
- Sound effects and music are "possibly the most important part" and the hardest; they decide whether an edit works [said 09:47]. Use I/O to pick just the wanted slice of an effect before dragging it to the timeline [said 10:28]; volume is set by dragging the clip's volume line [said 11:09].

## Against today's playbook
- retention.cutsPerMinute 12 / averageShotLength 5: this video measures 13.7-14.0 cuts/min and 4.2-4.3 s, so the playbook is close. [measured]
- retention.popupsPerMinute 1.0: this video shows a new meme/stock cutaway about every 2-4 s (up to 30 per minute by eye, many are cutaways rather than text popups), far above 1 per minute. Only a count of text/graphic pop-ups is comparable and that is not measured here; flag as a likely under-estimate for his style. [seen 00:00-01:00]
- retention.captions.wordsOnScreen 3 / uppercase true / positionY 0.82: matches in spirit (1-2 uppercase words, white with black outline); the one measured text position is 0.79 (850/1080) for a pop-up word [seen 06:00].
- smooth.zoomRamp 0.6 / retention.zoomRamp 0.16: he only says "a couple of frames" between zoom keyframes plus motion blur [said 03:54, 04:14]. At 60 fps that would be about 0.03-0.05 s; at 30 fps about 0.07 s. No timing number given for the zoom ramp itself; do not treat as confirmation.
- Transition length: whip pan adjustment layer "about 12 frames" [said 08:02]. At 60 fps = 0.20 s, at 30 fps = 0.40 s. The playbook has no whip-pan length; nothing to compare except fadesPerMinute, which is not what a whip pan is.
- retention principle "Sound sells the hit": he calls sound the most important part [said 09:47], and a transition with no good blur should lean on the whoosh [said 08:26]. Confirms.
