## Rules stated
- "Cinematic" is broken into five parts: storytelling (clear beginning, middle, end), visuals (composition, lighting, camera movement), sound, effects (CGI or practical), and the experience (how the audience feels) [said 01:22-03:27].
- Sound is "just as important as composition"; cinematic sound means a high-quality, immersive mix of music, dialogue and sound effects that raises the emotion of a scene [said 02:44].
- Rule of thirds: imagine a 3x3 grid, nine equal parts, and place key elements on the lines or intersections [said 04:09].
- Interview framing: put the subject off-centre when they talk to someone off-camera; leave "look space" in the direction they are looking so it does not feel awkward [said 04:29-04:49].
- Leading lines (paths, aisles, hallways, stairs) guide the eye to the subject; frame-within-a-frame (doors, windows, arches) adds depth and focus; shadows add depth and atmosphere, best on a sunny day with hard light [said 04:49-06:13].
- Edit with purpose: cut and sequence shots thoughtfully, add transitions, consider colour grading. Do not over-plan the edit while shooting when starting out [said 06:13-06:34]. No durations, counts or ramp times are given.
- Break the rules for creative effect; unconventional compositions can make a scene stand out [said 06:56].
- Lighting: start with natural light and watch how it changes through the day; use an ND filter, a diffuser or a reflector to control it [said 07:38-08:19].
- Front light is even but can look flat; side light gives depth and texture (expose for the shadow side when a subject sits by a window); backlight gives a halo or silhouette [said 08:40-09:00].
- Mood: warm, soft light reads cozy; cold, harsh light reads tense [said 09:20].
- Practice and watch films with a critical eye; do not stall on "where do I start" [said 10:01-10:43].
