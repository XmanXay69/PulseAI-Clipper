## Rules stated
- Editing is "invisible storytelling"; knowing the types of cut is key [said 00:00].
- The cut: an instant switch from one shot to the next, audio included; the most common cut by far. Example: a clean cut from a bar scene to an office scene [said 00:21].
- Jump cut: a piece of a shot removed and the ends joined, so the shot appears to jump in time; common in talking-head videos to drop pauses and mistakes; in a film it can show time passing and that people talked longer than the screen time [said 00:42-01:03].
- Match cut: a cut that matches action, shape, colour, framing or audio between two shots to carry the viewer seamlessly into the next scene; three types: graphic, movement (camera, character or object), audio [said 01:03-01:24].
- J cut: the audio of the next scene starts before the picture, so the viewer hears what is coming before seeing it [said 01:44-02:05].
- L cut: the audio of the current scene carries on after the picture cuts to the next, bridging the two [said 02:05-02:25].
- Why split edits: smoother, more natural flow, especially in conversation; they guide attention, build emotional impact and make a scene feel more immersive than a hard stop [said 02:53].
- Fade: a shot goes to or from a solid colour; black is the usual one, white for a death or a dream; typically at the start or end of a film to mark beginning or end [said 03:16].
- Dissolve: fade straight into a new shot instead of a colour; typically shows time passing within or between scenes [said 03:37].
- Cut on action: cut during a movement so that the out point of shot A matches the in point of shot B for a seamless join; very common and makes cuts smoother and more natural [said 03:57-04:18].
- Cross cutting: alternate between scenes that happen at the same time (thrillers, a two-person phone call) to tell two stories at once [said 04:38-04:58].
- Smash cut: a harsh, abrupt cut with contrasting picture and sound, such as a loud chaotic scene cutting to a quiet simple one [said 04:58-05:22].
- Plan and storyboard your cuts before shooting to get their full impact [said 05:22].

## Against today's playbook (smooth: cutsPerMinute 6, averageShotLength 10, fadesPerMinute 0.6, zoomRamp 0.6, "match moves across a cut")
- Adds: J cut and L cut as a named smoothing device in conversations (audio leads or trails the picture) so a hard cut "never feels like a stop" [said 02:53]. The playbook has no audio-lead rule; the video gives no size for the lead.
- Fades and dissolves are for time passing and for the start/end of a piece, with white only for death or dream [said 03:16-03:37]. This supports a low fadesPerMinute (0.6 is plausible) and suggests fades belong at section boundaries, not scattered.
- Cut on action: out point of A matches in point of B [said 03:57]. It is the same idea as "match moves across a cut"; the playbook wording is about zoom speed but the tutor means matching the action itself.
- Smash cut (abrupt, contrasting) is the intentional exception to "nothing snaps"; it is a deliberate break, used rarely [said 04:58].
- The tutorial's own 6-9 cuts/min with 6-7 s shots sits around the cinematic 6 cuts/min / 10 s target, but the measurement is of an explainer with film clips, not a cinematic piece [measured].
