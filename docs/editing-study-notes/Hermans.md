## Rules stated
- "Cinematic" is personal: colours, music, composition and putting something of yourself and some emotion in the work matter most; what looks cinematic to him may not to you [said 01:25-01:48].
- Arrange the clips in the order that flows before editing, but you can still move them as you go [said 01:48-02:09].
- Speed ramp: the clip plays at normal speed, speeds up, and returns to normal speed. Built in the Speed > Curve panel; he always uses the custom curve rather than a preset so he can match the ramp to the pace of the music [said 02:09-02:52].
- Drag the middle point up to speed the middle part; add another point to speed more of the clip, then keep tweaking the points and listening to the music until the ramp matches the music's pace. Normal at the start, a max-speed stretch, then back to normal [said 03:17-03:43]. No percentage is spoken; the on-screen axis gives it (see below).
- Main edit: edit the clips to the beat of the music. Auto Beats marks the beats with yellow dots; "B2" shows more beats and is the one he usually picks. You do not cut on every beat because it feels robotic; where to cut depends on what you want to show and what flows [said 04:07-04:57].
- Sound effects: a whoosh where the speed ramp happens, to make the ramp feel more dramatic; plus beach/ocean sounds along the whole sequence, dropped on new tracks under the music [said 05:22-05:43].
- Colour grading is optional; a filter ("Miami") dropped on the timeline becomes an adjustment clip that grades every clip under it, and can be lengthened to cover the sequence [said 06:07-06:33].
- Manual grade for a sunset look: temperature toward yellow, a bit more saturation, a bit more contrast, a tiny amount of illumination [said 06:56-07:21].
- Target colours in HSL: raise the saturation of yellows and oranges so the light pops, "not too much" or it looks unnatural [said 07:41].
- Curves: pull the shadows down and highlights up for an S-curve contrast; in the colour curves he adds blue in shadows and highlights only, locking the mid-tones with anchor points [said 08:07-09:15].
- "No rules", but do not overdo it; keep the image natural [said 09:15].

## Against today's playbook (smooth: cutsPerMinute 6, averageShotLength 10, zoomRamp 0.6, musicBed 0.95, effectsPerMinute 2, "whooshes on motion", "let the music carry it")
- Contradiction on shot length for a cinematic montage: the finished 30 s travel sequence is about 13 clips, about 2.3 s average, about 26 cuts/min, with cuts every 1.2 s or 2.4 s on a 0.6 s beat grid [seen 05:06, measured 10-38 s]. The playbook's 6 cuts/min / 10 s is closer to this video's talking-head part. If "smooth" is meant for beat-led b-roll, its numbers are about four times too slow; the `beat` playbook (22 cuts/min, 2.7 s) is nearer to what this cinematic montage does.
- Confirms "let the music carry it" and adds a concrete rule: cut on the music's beats but not every beat (every 2nd or 4th) because every beat feels robotic [said 04:31-04:57].
- Speed ramp with eased shoulders: 1x to about 10x and back, shaped on the curve to fit the music [seen 03:46, said 02:52]. The playbook has no speed-ramp rule for `smooth`. A ramp in this video compressed 13 s to 8 s, was bracketed by normal speed, and carried a whoosh.
- Supports "whooshes on motion": one whoosh of about 1.7 s on the speed ramp and a continuous ocean ambience bed under the whole 30 s [seen 05:45, said 05:22]. Effect count: about 2 layers in 30 s; as a rate that is about 2-4/min, consistent with effectsPerMinute 2 with the bed counted separately.
- Supports musicBed 0.95: the music track runs the whole sequence [seen 05:06].
- Ease timing is not stated in seconds, so zoomRamp 0.6 is neither confirmed nor refuted.

## Caveats

- The 0.35 threshold can miss dissolves and similar-looking shots, so the sequence may have more cuts than counted.
- The curve reading and slider identities are partly from the narration, since the labels were cropped or small.
- Not heard: music level, whoosh level. Frame times are the scene-detector's pick.
