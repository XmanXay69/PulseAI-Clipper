## Rules stated
- Opening claim: low-effort, poorly edited gaming videos are the ones to stop making; the video promises to show editing "like a pro" in CapCut [said 00:00].
- Record the facecam on a separate camera from the gameplay capture so there is more control over how much face versus gameplay is shown (his rig: capture card, separate mirrorless camera, desktop mic) [said 01:00-01:20].
- Put gameplay on the lower track, facecam above; sync them in one click with "sync video to sound" so the two audio waveforms line up [said 02:01-02:42].
- Make each track a compound clip so face and game can be edited separately; resize the facecam, crop it from wide 1080p to "slightly more square", and apply a rectangle mask [said 03:02-04:03].
- Colour-correct per clip type; he does not like applying one correction to everything unless all clips were recorded the same way [said 04:44-05:05].
- After correcting, drop the facecam's own audio (commentary is already in the gameplay clip) and group face + game so they stay together and cut as one [said 05:05-05:25].
- Cut out everything unrelated to the video; keep only the most important or most entertaining parts of what was recorded "so something is always interacting or engaging the viewer" [said 05:25-05:45].
- Tools: select and split (B key) to set start/end points; main-track magnet closes gaps (but group stacked clips first so they move together); preview-skim makes it easy to place the playhead [said 05:45-07:06].
- Dynamism: go back through the video and blow the facecam up from its small side position to larger at chosen moments, using a keyframe pair (scale 122% and, later in the same move, 144%, see below) [said 07:06-07:46].

## Caveats
- Software tutorial for one editor (CapCut); its "pro" advice is mostly how to find features, not why. The video opens like a product pitch and shows the "Join Pro" button; no sponsorship disclosure was in the captions, but treat praise of the tool as promotional.
- The example is a narrated guide with a facecam, not a funny highlight reel, so its low cut and effect rates are not a counter-example for fast comedic gaming edits.
- Spoken numbers come from auto captions: "18 times" is the caption's reading of a 1.8x setting I could see on screen, and "minus 23" has no unit stated (the app shows dB for volume, so −23 dB is likely but not shown).
- The zoom keyframe end time and the whole-timeline segment count are approximate (small frames); the 38% scale reading is from one cropped panel.
- Music and SFX loudness of the video's own audio were not measured.
