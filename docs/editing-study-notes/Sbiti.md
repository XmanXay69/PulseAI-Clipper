## Rules stated
- The sample short is vertical 9:16; the podcast clips (16:9) are all scaled to the same size by typing 198% in the scale field, leaving black bars above and below; he says you can go full-screen instead but keeps the bars [said 05:29-06:12].
- Put the best, most hard-hitting clip first, because the first clip decides whether people keep watching; he picked a clip that opens on a bold, slightly confusing line ("Champions are broken") as the hook [said 06:12-07:14].
- A clip that is already in circulation is not necessarily bad: it shows it works; he still tries something different to test [said 07:14-07:34].
- Cut the fluff at the head, tail and in rambling middle sections so the clip starts exactly on the first strong words (his example starts on "driven human beings"); do it before anything else because tangents lose the viewer [said 05:29, 07:34-07:58].
- Add one adjustment layer across all clips for a house look: brightness down about -5 to -10 (he does not have a fixed amount), contrast up about +5, shadows down a bit [said 08:38-09:00]. Check no clip is too dark or too bright and trim individual clips: one clip got an extra -5, i.e. -11 where the others sat at -6 [said 09:20-09:41].
- Optional desaturation for a branded look; he used black-and-white at first and moved to colour [said 09:00].
- Captions: either auto-captions or a single reaction word. With only the word "Real" (a relatable clip) a video can be edited "within the space of 10 minutes" [said 10:01-10:21].
- Auto-captions: make them really small so they do not get in the way (small text makes people look closer), keep them on one line (never two), and put them where they miss every speaker's face: just under the chin and above the chest, the same place for all clips; split lines in the caption list if one runs long [said 10:21-12:25].
- Instagram: use auto-captions rather than "Real" (he found "Real" alone does worse there) [said 19:15-19:35].
- "Real" style text: small, not bold, thin stroke, shadow, left in the middle of the frame, dragged across the whole video [said 14:27-14:47].
- Music: it must not be louder than the speakers and not too quiet; he lands on -11 dB for the clip's volume ("sounds about right throughout"); the level meter should stay between -12 and 0 for the whole video; test candidate tracks by playing them under the video [said 13:06-13:46].
- End: a logo card with a quote (5 to 6 seconds), logo at about 25% opacity behind the quote, fade the picture in and out, fade the logo/quote out "about 1 second" and the music out over 2 seconds; he leaves the music a little longer on a black screen "a bit emotional" [said 15:08-17:52]. The quote is there to show added value and avoid "unoriginal content" strikes in TikTok's creativity programme [said 15:29-15:50].
- Proofread the final: no extra caption words, no parts louder or quieter than others [said 17:52-18:13].
- Export 1080p at 30 fps ("no need to do more"); the caption reads "180p" but the dialog shows 1080P [said 18:33-18:55].
- Platform rules (not editing craft): post from the phone rather than a laptop; draft 24 h before posting so TikTok processes the upload; put a sound on top of the sound so a muted video still has an audio track [said 18:13-21:17].

## Against today's playbook
- `short` playbook cutsPerMinute 18 / averageShotLength 3.3 / zoomsPerMinute 9 / effectsPerMinute 4: this popular podcast-clip format does the opposite. His sample is about 3.2 cuts/min (clips of 10 to 31 s), no zooms, no sound effects, no transitions [seen 08:43, 13:46, 17:32]. It supports "podcast clips cut for social" being far calmer than the 18-cuts/min short; the retention-style short does not apply.
- `short` captions wordsOnScreen 2, maxCaptionWords 3, positionY 0.68, lineHeight 0.07: he uses a full line of auto-caption text in tiny type (font size 8) at about the 50% line, always one line, with the explicit rule "never two lines" [said 11:44, seen 11:33]. That supports one line and a placement just under the chin, but contradicts large 1-2-word captions for this format.
