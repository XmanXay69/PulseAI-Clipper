## Rules stated
- The workflow has ten steps and must be run for every video: import/organise (A-roll and B-roll folders), lay the story foundation in order, colour, sound, cut the fluff, b-roll, graphics, effects, music and sound effects, export; the b-roll/graphics/effects steps (6, 7, 8) can be done one at a time or all in one longer pass [said 00:42, 01:22-02:03, 11:56-12:16].
- Do colour and sound first, on whole clips, before cutting, so the rest of the edit is not done on a flat grey image; for log footage use a conversion LUT, lift exposure a little, shadows up if they are dark, highlights down, "a hair of contrast", and use apply-to-all [said 02:03-05:49].
- Audio per clip: tick normalise loudness and reduce noise; there is no apply-to-all for audio, so repeat it per clip [said 06:09-06:50].
- Cut the fluff = unwanted pauses, mistakes and retakes, boring sections; unless you are perfect on camera they will kill retention [said 07:52].
- Enlarge the waveform view to 60% to see where speech is; split with Cmd/Ctrl+B, delete left with Q, delete right with W; he says these three shortcuts speed editing "like crazy" and not to click the buttons [said 08:12-10:35].
- Transcript editing: highlight text or a pause marker in the transcript and delete; the example pause was 12.42 seconds [said 10:55-11:35; seen 11:12 "12.42s"].
- B-roll: a more visually varied video is better; the creators he follows keep breaking up the pattern, and one covers his talking with "as much" b-roll as possible [said 12:16-12:57]. List the b-roll you need after the rough edit and shoot it in one outing [said 13:18].
- Put b-roll on the layer above the A-roll; the bottom layer is for the talking portion only; the highest layer always gets visual precedence [said 14:59-15:21]. Mark in/out on the source so only the wanted portion lands on the timeline, then trim [said 14:18-14:59].
- Stabilise shaky b-roll with the stabilise tick (processing is automatic) [said 16:22-16:42; seen 16:48].
- Transitions between A-roll and b-roll: "pull in" and "pull out" zooms are "one of the cleanest and most professional"; the clips must be touching on one track; turn off the track magnet so lifting a clip does not ripple the others [said 17:02-19:25].
- Text pop-ups: one word per text layer, placed on the word as it is spoken; set an in-animation (he chose Glitch, stays "around one second") and an out-animation (slide down), then alt-drag the layer to copy style and animations; tighten the gaps afterwards because his first placement was "way too slow" [said 20:07-23:09].
- Graphic card: a moving-gradient background clip, a slide transition into it, an image animated with a Roll preset, then the two text lines; "it only took 30 seconds" [said 23:49-26:33]. To put a transition on a stack of layers, select them and make a compound clip, then drop a glare transition [said 26:54-27:36].
- Sound design: a riser or hit marks each new section and whooshes and pops go with graphics; you may not consciously hear each one [said 28:58-29:18]. Add effects "at every opportunity possible" [said 32:44-33:05].
- Sound-effect handling: drop a whoosh on the wipe; lower its volume; cut a long glitch sound down to the length of the text pop; add a short fade-in by dragging the fade dot so it is less harsh [said 30:59-32:44].
- Music: bring it down to "usually around -20 dB" (louder songs lower), play the video through to check it is not jarring; he breaks the video into chapters with a new song for each segment rather than one song throughout [said 33:46-34:28].
- Final pass: watch it all, stopping to fix colour on b-roll missed earlier; check text and graphics for typos ("one or two always squeak through") [said 34:49-35:11].

## Against today's playbook
- commentary.musicBed 0.25 / principle "music far under the talking or none": he sets music at about -20 dB and sound effects around -12 dB on the clip faders [said 33:46, seen 34:10, 32:40]; his own video keeps music nearly continuous and changes the song per chapter [said 34:28]. This supports "far under the voice", but not "or none" for a YouTube video.
- commentary.effectsPerMinute 0.2 / maxEffectsPerMinute 1.5: this creator puts sound effects on every transition and graphic, i.e. far above 1.5/min, and calls it the way to add depth [said 32:44-33:05]. The commentary numbers are not supported for retention-style YouTube; they could still be right for calm talk.
- commentary.averageShotLength 20 / cutsPerMinute 3: after cutting the fluff his A-roll pieces are 7 to 25 s [seen 15:05], which is consistent with the playbook, but he then covers the talk with a b-roll insert of about 5.5 s per beat, and the finished video measures 10 to 12 cuts/min [measured 0:00-3:00]. Playbook numbers match his raw talking portions, not his finished pace.
- pauseRatio 0.11: he removes pauses outright ("only the talking portions"), a 12.42 s pause as the example [said 09:14, 11:15]. Supports a low pause ratio; no number.
- zoomRamp 0.4 / zoomScale 1.08: not supported or contradicted; his zooms are transition presets (pull in/out), and the one transition duration shown is 0.5 s (Glare II) [seen 27:45]. His text in-animation is "around one second" [said 21:48].
- hookDeadline 20 s: the promise is stated in the first 20 seconds [caption 00:01-00:21]; supported.
- popupsPerMinute 0.05: not supported for this style; one-word pop-ups are a tutorial topic and his own video shows a pop-up graphic or title card every few seconds in the intro [seen 00:06-01:23].
- principle "Hide the join": supported by "b-roll covers the cut" but with no punch-in mention; his rule is to put the insert on the layer above so the A-roll stays untouched [said 14:59-15:21].
