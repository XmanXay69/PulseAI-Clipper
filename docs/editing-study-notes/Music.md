## Rules stated
- The point of the video: music-video editing tricks can strengthen specific moments in any video, gaming included; he is not asking for music videos [said 00:00 to 00:30].
- Never use any song you like: YouTube's handling of copyrighted music means a claim on a 10 minute video over 10 seconds of background music takes all the revenue (his example figures: "10 minute video", "10 seconds") [said 01:00 to 01:21].
- Looking at other videos' descriptions for an auto-generated music box is not a reliable licence check; his verdict on it is "basically no" and "don't touch it" (the caption wording is ambiguous about which way the box points) [said 01:21 to 01:40].
- Uploading as unlisted lets YouTube pre-check, but do not rely on it: it misses claims "one percent" of the time until after publishing [said 01:42 to 02:04].
- Best fix: pay for a licensed music library (he names Epidemic Sound) or use his own free essentials playlist [said 01:42 to 02:04].
- Cut on the beat. It is common in TV, film and YouTube; in a gaming video it works as a "subconscious" effect that adds to flow, and viewers will not notice, which is the aim [said 02:25 to 02:45].
- Method: drag the music folder into the project, place the track on the timeline, tick the audio-preview-while-scrubbing option in Preferences so each frame step plays a snippet, then step through and move cuts so they land on beats [said 02:25 to 03:25].
- Cutting to the beat lifts flow "from a two out of ten to a three out of ten" (a joke figure; he shows 2/10 then 3/10 on screen) [said 03:25, seen 03:25 tile].
- Do not cut on odd beats that make no sense. Any beat is better than none, but chosen deliberately is better [said 03:25 to 03:45].
- Blend-mode layering ("trippy" look): duplicate the clip above itself, set the top clip's blend mode to Lighten, scale it up, add a circular mask, invert it, raise mask expansion and feathering; duplicate the result and repeat; a colour preset finishes it [said 03:50 to 04:32].
- Speed ramp: enlarge the track, right-click the clip, Time Remapping, Speed. Pen tool (P) to click where the slow-down or speed-up begins; the speed line splits into two halves; V and drag up or down to set each side's speed; the first result looks choppy, so pull the keyframe halves apart and drag the Bezier handles to smooth it [said 04:32 to 05:23].
- For a spike in speed add another keyframe beside the first and shape it; he notes this is the Fortnite-montage look [said 05:23 to 05:43].
- Finish a speed ramp with a flash on the peak: new colour matte, white, shorten to "about five frames", opacity keyframes 0, then 100, then 0 with the middle keyframe exactly on the peak [said 05:43 to 06:12].
- If music is on the timeline, sync that flash so it lands on a beat; the layered effect can be repeated after the flash [said 06:12 to 06:22].
- Not stated: no music volume under speech, no ducking, no fade in or out lengths, no dB figure at all, no sound effects per minute, no meme rule. His edit never discusses voice over music.
