## Rules stated
- Text pop animation recipe (credited to the YouTuber Finzar): Transform effect on the text, Scale keyframe at the start of about 70%, go forward about 7 frames and set 110%, go forward another 5 frames and set 100%; smooth the keyframes ("not a big difference"); add glow or glitch; save as a preset [said 13:28-13:49]. The sequence timecodes on screen (00:13:56:43, 13:56:50, 13:56:55) are consistent with the 7-frame and 5-frame steps, and Scale 70.0 is visible at the 13:56:50 position; the 110 and 100 values were not legible in my frames [seen 13:37-13:50].
- Zoom in/out recipe: Transform effect, scale and position keyframes at the start, move forward, set the end; then open the keyframes and smooth the curves (Bezier ease) because the raw version "is not smooth at all" [said 12:00-12:24].
- Keep the focus point in the same spot across cuts and zooms (centre, eye line, or rule of thirds guides; his favourite is thirds) so the eye does not wander [said 12:24-12:44].
- Captions: use them where something important is said; avoid text popping every second, it becomes irritating. Three kinds of text: caption, centre text (own colour/glow) and text attached to something in the scene [said 12:44-13:28].
- Music: lay it down right after the voice-over, before the pictures; gives the vibe and lets you cut on the beat. Cuts on the music beat are "not necessary if you're new" but "give that extra punch" [said 06:50, 07:36]. Set music volume against the voice-over [said 07:13].
- Pace vs space: cut every moment where nothing interesting happens, but do not cut the breathing room the story needs; "people don't have shorter attention spans, they have higher standards", so make each pause worth waiting for [said 10:54-11:39].
- Good editing is effects that complement what is said, not as many as possible [said 11:39]; "just because you can use an effect doesn't mean you should" [said 15:44].
- Sound effects: three basic kinds, whoosh, riser, impact; add keyboard, clicks, clock, etc. as needed [said 16:05]. Add them with the other audio tracks muted; combine several sounds; lower their volume so they are not annoying; sound design is "literally half of the video" [said 16:28-16:52].
- Colour: a Lumetri Color effect on each clip (exposure, contrast, saturation) [said 16:52].
- Overlays (light leaks, electric, dust) set to Screen blend, mostly used as transitions; also preset packs for slides, shakes, zooms, rotations; his effect list is glow, 3D, transform, blur, mosaic and he says he does not go heavy [said 14:36-15:19].
- Project: 1920x1080 at 60 fps [said 06:08]. Voice-over cut to the script with awkward pauses removed [said 06:29].
- Do a second pass: add animations, screen recordings, memes, and remove lines/footage/jokes that do not serve the main goal [said 10:32-10:54].
- Colour-code the script into 6 categories (screen record, animation, etc.) and gather assets per sentence before opening the editor [said 05:27].
- Two ways to animate: Canva for simple ones (export higher quality if you will zoom into them), After Effects for typewriter text, trim paths and glow [said 08:47-09:51].
- Not much on structure for shorts; mentions a "secondary story" alongside the main one [said 03:11].

## Against today's playbook
Comparison is with punchyShort (for the text pop and sound design) and onTheBeat (for the beat remark). The video is long-form, so cut-rate numbers are not compared.
- zoomRamp 0.12 (punchyShort) and 0.1 (onTheBeat): his text pop goes 70% to 110% in 7 frames at 60 fps = 0.117 s, then settles to 100% in 5 more frames = 0.083 s [said 13:36-13:49; seen 13:56:43-13:56:55]. Supports a 0.1-0.12 s ramp for a snap-in with a small overshoot; the overshoot (110%) and the 70% start are new numbers. His zoom-in on a picture is an eased Bezier move that he calls necessary "to be smooth", not a snap [said 12:24].
- onTheBeat "cut on the kick and the snare": he treats cutting to the beat as optional, "an extra punch", for general edits [said 07:36]. Weak support only.
- Principle "A flash or a shake on every beat stops meaning anything": echoed by "not as many effects as you can" [said 11:39, 15:44].
- Effects per minute: nothing numeric; SFX types whoosh/riser/impact confirm the effect set; SFX volume lowered and designed with other tracks muted, which the playbook does not mention [said 16:28-16:52].
- Captions (punchyShort coverage 0.95): he captions only important lines in long-form, opposite of shorts; not evidence against the short value [said 12:44-13:05].
- pauseRatio: voice-over fragments with removed gaps [seen 14:29] and "cut every moment where nothing interesting happens" with a counterweight for breathing room [said 11:16-11:39].
