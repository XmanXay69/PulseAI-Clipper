## Rules stated
- Learn the software by organising: rename the clips in the project panel, treat it like Finder/Explorer; this was "easy" and the quickest step [said 02:02-02:43].
- Build the structure of the video by putting named clips on the timeline in order; pacing came naturally to the beginner (no numbers) [said 02:43].
- Voice-over: the record button gives a 3-second countdown; do many takes because "we can cut it up later" [said 03:03-03:24].
- Adjustment-layer transition (slide up): drag the adjustment layer's end until it is "like 12 frames long", put effects on that one layer, then highlight the finished transition and duplicate it for the others instead of rebuilding [said 04:46-05:07]. The project in view runs at 59.97 fps [seen 04:32], so 12 frames is 0.20 s.
- Tracking: key-frame a picture onto a moving building (the "finzar HQ" sticker); a flashy preset animation is then added to the nested sequence [said 04:04-04:25].
- Rotoscoping in After Effects (Rotoscope 3.0) puts text behind the person; first pass needed only one or two corrections [said 06:30-06:51].
- Subtitles take the longest and need the most general Premiere knowledge [said 07:13]. Press T for text and duplicate [said 07:33]. Give each person their own subtitle colour; he notes she used "some light color theory" [said 07:13-07:33]. The subtitle pass took more than an hour on top of the first stretch [said 08:54].
- Music: choose by trying the classics against the picture; they used one song he has used "a lot" on his channel; then set the audio gain [said 09:16].
- Sound effects: the main lesson was "to be efficient": Option-drag to duplicate whooshes, set gain so they are "not too intrusive", and choose each effect for what is on screen [said 09:36-09:56].
- Order of work as shown: organise, timeline, voice-over, trim to the voice-over, first draft (clock time 11:49), tracking and animation, transitions (clock time 12:30), rotoscoping, subtitles, music, sound effects, final tweaks [said 03:44, 05:07].
