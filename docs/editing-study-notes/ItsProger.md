## Rules stated
- Clean editing is what keeps viewers (retention span is low) [said 00:00].
- Learn your workspace and the keybinds; it roughly halves the time everything takes [said 00:00-00:41].
- Rough cut first from A-roll (you talking to camera) and B-roll (shows what you say); use "three to four times as much B-roll as A-roll" because looking at your face or your Minecraft character is not interesting [said 00:41].
- B-roll sources: free or paid sites, or record it yourself with a replay mod, a cinematic mod and OBS (promo for a stock marketplace follows) [said 00:41-01:43].
- Subtitles raise retention because they make people read; make them fast: press T, type, pick an easy-to-read font with a stroke and maybe a shadow [said 02:04-02:25].
- Add subtitles to the intro and to important moments, not to every line (a duplicated text layer via Alt-drag, reworded) [said 03:05].
- Pop-up text animation by keyframes on scale: first keyframe a bit smaller than final, a few frames forward a keyframe a bit bigger than the start, and another couple of frames forward back to normal size [said 02:45-03:05]. The values on screen are 82.0, then 103.0, then 100.0 [seen 03:04-03:10].
- Transitions bring life between clips, but do not add too many; sometimes none is better and too many look messy; a good place to start is at changes of topic, music or chapter [said 03:26-03:47].
- Slide-in: key position on screen at the time it should be there, go back, add a key with the object off screen, then shape the velocity graph (one shape for in, one for out, one for normal) [said 04:30-04:50].
- Zoom-in animation on a graphic: first key on scale smaller than the end size (he picks 50), a second key a bit later at full size; zoom-out is the same with the keys swapped; vary the velocity graphs so it does not look robotic [said 05:10-05:30].
- Motion blur: push the shutter angle slider all the way up, and use 360 for the rotation move [said 05:30-05:50].
- Rotation: two keys a little apart, bigger rotation number = more turns; rotation velocity has two lines, so set both keys to Bezier and align the lower handles precisely [said 05:50-06:31].
- Save finished moves as presets (anchor to in point for entrances, out point for exits) and reuse them on text, objects or whole clips [said 06:51-07:11].
- Use the Transform effect, not the built-in Motion, for shutter-angle blur and so several moves can overlap and run into each other smoothly [said 07:11-07:32].
- Add drop shadows (a Minecraft-style one and a realistic one) for a lot of lift [said 07:32].
- Characters: make a circular avatar with inner glow and inner shadow, put it on a cut-out body with a drop shadow and a swinging "breathing" preset, nest the layers, then animate it in [said 08:12-09:14].
- Zooms: scale key just before the zoom and another a few frames later at the bigger scale; tune the velocity graph; slow zooms on still images, faster zooms to emphasise a point when saying something important [said 09:34-09:55].
- Tracking by hand: position keyframes through the clip to follow a head [said 10:16-10:36].
- Left for another video: sound effects, music, adjustment layers, colour correction, volume [said 10:36].
