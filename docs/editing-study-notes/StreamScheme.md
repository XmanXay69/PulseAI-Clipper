## Rules stated
- Every piece of content, "five seconds or five hours", follows hook, meat, payoff; the hook is the promise that a viewer should stay [said 02:33-02:55]. Plan the stream around it (his Q&A and Pokemon-Vegemite punishment examples) and treat a stream as a recording session [said 02:55-04:39].
- TikTok is a "retention rate game": the opening frame must hook and state the promise, and rewatches are powerful [said 05:27]. Hook formulas he names: "Did you know ...", "only hardcore gamers will understand this" [said 05:49].
- A caption added afterwards can serve as the hook: a clip of him looking foolish for "about eight seconds" with a text "I didn't read the instructions" got 6,400 views and 200 likes [said 06:10-06:33].
- Length: he rejects the "eight-second meta". Findings in order: a 17 s clip with a good hook got about 3,000 views and 151 likes in day one; five clips cut to 5-8 s each all got 300 views or less ("length"); a 17 s clip that opened "this is the hardest unknown to find" and then walked the route got 1,700 views and 126 likes; a 33 s clip opened with "99% of you don't know about this secret" got 28,600 views, 175 shares, 147 comments, 2,112 likes in a day and 78,000 views, 3,700 likes later [said 06:56-09:09]. Rule: make sure they watch the whole thing, but too short does not work for him [said 07:16].
- Unplanned funny clips are "hit and miss"; three Pokemon-guessing clips opened with "who's that Pokemon" got 2,300 to 2,700 views each [said 09:29-09:53]. A Wordle clip reached 500,000 views and 24,000 likes in about 12 hours, 1.4 million later [said 10:13-10:34]. A trimmed Elden Ring clip with "carefully timed" cutoff got 100,000 in day one [said 10:34].
- Cut 15 to 30 clips per stream; trim each as tight as possible before publishing; make the first moment of each engaging [said 12:04-12:26]. Use the Twitch clip tool, not the highlight tool [said 12:04].
- Layout: three layers, facecam, main gameplay, blurred background [said 13:35]. Cam can be square (corner radius 0) with a drop shadow offset "15 pixels on both axis" or a border (colour plus thickness in px) [said 14:22-14:45]. A saved per-game template makes each clip a one-click convert [said 16:15-16:58].
- Trim every layer individually; there was no "split all layers" [said 15:31].
- Finish in the TikTok app, not the web tool: add text such as "wait for it..." or "did you know", text-to-speech "if it fits", closed captions with swear words removed or hidden; sounds and extras kept "as simple as possible" so the content carries it [said 18:05-18:49]. One quick funny line as the caption, 3 to 5 hashtags aimed at large and medium volume [said 18:49]. Choose an eye-catching cover with big text [said 18:53]. Queue drafts and post one a day [said 19:11].
- Time budget: one or two streams give 30-plus clips for an hour or two of work afterwards [said 00:23-01:03, 16:15].
