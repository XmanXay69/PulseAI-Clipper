## Rules stated
- A viral edit needs four things: a trendy song, good clips, smooth speed ramps and great effects [said 00:00].
- Clips: screen-record "car 4K" videos from YouTube, but for the modern speed-ramp edits join Discord servers that share free clips (he names Sutra edits). Audio: download a trending sound from TikTok [said 00:20-01:00].
- Timeline: 60 frames per second for both timeline and playback; vertical resolution 1080x1920 for TikTok/Instagram [said 01:00-01:21]. On screen the dialog offers 1080x1920 and 2160x3840 and playback rate reads 60 [seen 01:25].
- Mark the beats by pressing M on the timeline (timeline markers on the ruler, not in the audio layer); then cut each clip to start at a beat marker, make it a compound clip, trim the rest [said 01:21-02:23]. If you run out of clips, copy clips from the timeline and reuse them [said 02:23].
- Speed ramp: open the compound clip in Fusion, add a Time Stretcher, keep a keyframe on the first frame, go to the last frame and raise the Source Time; in the spline window make a keyframe at each beat marker and shape the curve [said 02:46-03:27].
- The video must always speed up on a beat mark: each beat mark corresponds to a fast point on the graph [said 04:31].
- Make clips flow into each other: if clip one moves left to right, clip two should move the same way; for the second clip put a keyframe on the first frame, move the last-frame source time towards zero, select both points and press S to smooth the curve [said 03:27-03:49].
- "Boomerang" ramp: a keyframe at the second beat marker, then lift the graph between two keyframes to create a back-and-forth [said 04:10-04:31].
- Falling transition: copy the second clip, make it a compound clip, change speed to Freeze Frame, place it between clips; mask the car with the pen tool in the Color page with Alpha Output; in Fusion keyframe the Transform centre and angle: at the last frame the normal position, at the first frame the car out of frame at a different angle; adjust the curve; enable motion blur [said 05:29-06:35].
- Blink effect: copy the clip, mask and track the headlight, add a solid colour matching the car, animate its position with one keyframe at the start, middle and end so the lights look shut [said 06:56-08:08].
- Camera shake: adjustment layer, Fusion Camera Shake, turn Overall Strength down on the first frame (keyframe), go a few frames forward and turn it up, return to 0 at the end; Edges set to Mirror; add Directional Blur and keyframe Glow and Length like the shake; smooth all points with S; copy the shake to every point you want [said 08:28-09:31].
- Sound design is "crucial": whoosh sounds for speed ramps, explosion sounds for explosions, a cartoon blink sound for the blink; adjust timing and volume for balance and match each sound to its effect "very precisely" or the edit looks low quality; sounds from YouTube or Pixabay [said 09:52-10:14].
- Final touches: motion blur on for all animations and transitions; put all speed-ramped clips in one compound clip, in Fusion add Optical Flow then Vector Motion Blur with Scale 0.3; then grade the compound of everything except shakes: Color Boost 75, Shadows -65, Highlights 75, a curve, a second node with a Glow; "colour depends heavily on the clip" [said 10:14-11:37]. Render QuickTime / H.264 at the project resolution and frame rate [said 11:37].

## Against today's playbook
Compared with onTheBeat (cutsPerMinute 22, averageShotLength 2.7 s, zoomsPerMinute 4, effectsPerMinute 3, maxEffectsPerMinute 6, fadesPerMinute 0.3, zoomRamp 0.1, "Cut on the kick and snare", "Build, drop, breathe", "Slow down for the best one", "Hold back before the drop").
- averageShotLength 2.7 s / cutsPerMinute 22: his final edit measures gaps of 0.85-1.7 s, mean about 1.3 s, about 45 cuts/min; compound clips 0.65-1.6 s [measured 143-167 and 718-758 s; seen 08:40]. About double the frag-movie rate; the car/speed-ramp sub-style needs its own fingerprint.
- "Cut on the kick and the snare": his cuts land on a grid of about 0.42 s but only on every 2nd-4th beat, while the speed-ramp keyframes and the fast point of the ramp land on every beat marker [said 04:31; seen 02:55; measured]. Answers "every beat or every bar": cut every 2-4 beats (about 0.85-1.7 s), ramp on every beat.
- "Slow down for the best one: a time ramp into the play, then full speed": this style ramps every clip (about 1x to several x within 0.85-1.6 s, curve fastest at the beat) rather than one hero ramp; the "boomerang" back-and-forth is used for emphasis [said 04:10-04:54; seen 04:40]. No slow-down-to-slow-motion in this style.
- zoomRamp 0.1: shake attack about 7 frames = 0.12 s, decaying to 0 by frame 29, total 0.48 s [said 08:48; seen 09:00]; the falling transition is 21 frames = 0.35 s with an eased angle [seen 06:35]. Confirms a 0.1 s hit and adds a 0.4 s decay.
- effectsPerMinute 3 / maxEffectsPerMinute 6: about six effect clips (3 shakes, 2 falling transitions, 1 blink) in the first 6.5 s of his timeline, i.e. several times the playbook ceiling [seen 08:40, 10:00; estimated]. Contradicts the playbook for this sub-style, although each effect is tied to a beat and is not a shake on every beat.
- "A flash or a shake on every beat stops meaning anything": shakes are spaced by roughly 0.8-2.7 s in the frame I read (1.5, 2.3, 5.0 s), not on every 0.4 s beat [seen 10:00]. Supports "not every beat".
- fadesPerMinute 0.3 / hard cuts: no dissolves; transitions are masked "falling" moves, flashes and freeze-frames [said 05:29].
- "Hold back before the drop": not stated; the marker row in my two frames is dense at the start (4 markers in 1.1 s), then none from about 1.5 to 5 s, then dense again; that might be a held build but the clip boundaries at 2.4 and 3.3 s have no visible marker, so I do not treat it as evidence [seen 01:50, 05:45, 10:00].
- "No talking, no captions": consistent, he uses none.
- SFX: whoosh on speed ramps, explosion, blink sound, matched very precisely, is a new explicit rule; the playbook only gives counts [said 09:52].
