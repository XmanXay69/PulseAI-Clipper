## Rules stated
He frames it as eight steps for a video's introduction. No seconds, frame counts, dB or percentages are given anywhere in the video; the only numbers are the step numbers.
- The intro is the most important part of the edit after the title and thumbnail; a bad one makes the viewer leave, and the editor's job is both to say what the video is and to tempt people to finish it [said 00:10-00:39].
- Step 1, be inclusive: write the intro as if speaking to one imagined target viewer. Do not open on an inside joke or chat between friends that a stranger cannot follow (he calls that excluding the audience) [said 01:24-02:46].
- Step 2, say three things up front, "what, how and why": what you will do, how you will do it, why you are doing it (his example is building a Nether portal, via a dangerous trip for resources, to win a girl's affection) [said 02:46-03:07].
- Step 3, use graphics: PNGs, JPEGs and meme clips thrown around the screen, animated, so the intro is visually busy and the viewer does not get bored. Combine it with the what/how/why [said 03:07-03:28].
- Anti-pattern: a plain voice-over laid on a (maybe blurry) gameplay clip is "mind-numbingly boring" and wins fewer views [said 03:28].
- Never ask for likes or subscribes in the intro (stressed repeatedly) [said 03:48].
- Step 4, lots of sound effects in the intro: imagine everything moves through thick air that makes noise when it moves. Heavy SFX is for the intro, not necessarily the whole video. A low-energy voice-over with no SFX saps energy [said 03:48-04:30].
- Step 5, be confident on camera (no craft detail) [said 04:30].
- Step 6, take ideas from other channels' pacing and energy, but borrow small things, not the whole intro. Big regular channels all run formulas [said 04:50-05:30].
- Step 7, plan the moments right after the intro: do not drop from a great intro straight into dull gameplay. Stay inclusive past the intro and "ease out" the effort rather than stopping suddenly, which is grating and loses viewers [said 05:34-06:15]. This is the only smooth/ease idea in the video, and it is about energy, not motion.
- Step 8, music: pick a track with a steady beat to keep the pace going; the music is what makes the intro individual [said 06:15-06:35].
- Branding anti-pattern: a small logo over a blurry background is not an intro. Channels with millions of views can skip intros because they already have an audience; a growing channel has to earn the view [said 00:51-01:24].
